# Character foundation

Status: **canonical campaign-one ensemble under STORY-007 A**

All names, biographies, relationships, and visual hooks are fictional. They are not based on identifiable SMS InfoComm or Wistron employees. TASK-030's reviewed original character art is the canonical visual anchor for this ensemble; these prose notes remain its continuity and art-direction source rather than a substitute asset specification.

## Ensemble principle

No single character personifies an entire lifecycle stage. Each person has a home function, but competent depot work overlaps. The cast becomes interesting when two specialists interpret the same Evidence through different responsibilities.

Every recurring character should provide at least three things:

1. a human relationship to one part of the workflow;
2. a credible source of knowledge, friction, or access;
3. a life and pressure that do not disappear when the player's Ticket closes.

## The player-character

### Crossline Technician, Continuity Rotation

**Home team:** First Look<br>
**Player-selected:** name, appearance, pronouns, portrait, and later cosmetic identity<br>
**Authored:** job position, campaign access, professional growth, and consequential relationships

The protagonist is early in their Second Current career but not technically helpless. They know how to handle hardware safely, follow procedures, and ask a useful first question. What they do not yet understand is how a production depot distributes knowledge and accountability.

The player begins with a common technician's temptation: if the symptom is gone, the work feels done. The Continuity Rotation teaches that a repair must survive independent Verify, a handoff, a Worklog, shipment, and actual client use.

This model keeps the campaign personal without forcing every player into one authored face. Dialogue can address the protagonist by selected short name or neutral role. A small set of response attitudes—direct, curious, careful, or dry—could add texture later without creating divergent technical truth.

**Portrait direction:** customizable technician framed consistently with the ensemble; Second Current workwear, visible ESD practice, one selected personal object, and a background accent reflecting the current rotation. Avoid making identity options confer inherent technical power.

## Core ensemble

### Evelyn “Ev” Shaw — Core Floor supervisor

**Home function:** Operations leadership; formerly Failure Analysis<br>
**Story role:** sponsor of the Continuity Rotation and the person accountable when its discoveries become politically inconvenient

Ev can read a floor from the pattern of empty benches. She rose through Trace, then accepted management because she was tired of correct analyses arriving after operational decisions had already been made. She speaks in short questions and lets silence expose weak reasoning.

She believes the company can improve, but she has survived enough improvement programs to know that a pilot can become a slogan without changing anyone's authority. Her contradiction is that she asks the player to cross silos while sometimes withholding context to protect the program.

**Relationship to the loop:** Ev protects the end-to-end sequence and asks what evidence justifies moving forward. She is most likely to send a Ticket backward after failed Verify.

**Campaign pressure:** if the rotation slows visible throughput, Ev may lose the staff and access that make it possible. If she conceals its findings, it has no purpose.

**Portrait hook:** early fifties; compact stance; charcoal floor jacket over an old Trace shirt; silver at the temples; an obsolete pocket oscilloscope kept as a reminder rather than a useful tool; cool steel-blue accent.

**Voice sample:** “Tell me what changed. Then tell me what you only hoped would change.”

### Inez Calder — Inflow lead

**Home function:** Returns, receiving, and chain of custody<br>
**Story role:** the first person to see every client story before the building translates it into technical work

Inez remembers packaging the way other people remember faces. A crushed corner, doubled security seal, fresh label over an old route, or missing accessory can tell her which part of a complaint belongs to transit, field handling, or the machine itself.

She is fast, funny, and unimpressed by technicians who call dock information “just logistics.” Her blind spot is that experience sometimes lets her form a conclusion faster than she can explain it to someone who did not share the observation.

**Relationship to the loop:** Observe, identity, provenance, and the boundary between admissible context and speculation.

**Campaign pressure:** the Civic Atlas rush contract rewards speed at receiving, exactly when repeat serials and inconsistent condition notes need more attention.

**Portrait hook:** high-visibility dock jacket over an antistatic polo; handheld scanner; dark curls tied back with a bright orange band; cargo doors and pallet silhouettes; orange and navy accent.

**Voice sample:** “Machines arrive with two histories: the one in the case file and the one on the box.”

### Malik Okoye — Test systems engineer

**Home function:** Rigline automation and test-profile development<br>
**Story role:** teaches the player that a result is only as meaningful as the fixture, condition, target, and question that produced it

Malik can make a wall of noisy hardware produce clean structured data. He enjoys automation because it frees people from repetitive work, not because he believes it removes judgment. His recurring refrain is that test rigs do not lie, but fixtures, profiles, thresholds, and interpretations can all be wrong.

He is generous with anyone who wants to understand a tool and impatient with people who want a green badge without reading the conditions. His contradiction is professional pride: when a Civic Atlas unit passes his standard profile and fails in the field, he initially defends reproducibility before asking whether the profile reproduces the right world.

**Relationship to the loop:** Test selection, repeat executions, controlled conditions, negative results, and Evidence quality.

**Campaign pressure:** contract management wants a faster profile; Trace wants richer captures; the floor wants both.

**Portrait hook:** late thirties; rolled sleeves; hearing protection resting at the neck; cable labels aligned with almost comic precision; test rack light reflected in glasses; teal accent.

**Voice sample:** “The green light answers the question we gave it. That does not make it the question the client asked.”

### Sora Chen — Failure Analysis engineer

**Home function:** Trace<br>
**Story role:** the player's mentor in causal reasoning and the strongest opponent of premature certainty

Sora works comfortably at several scales: client history, event log, board view, rail measurement, solder joint, and component lot. She is known for drawing small fault trees on anything left near her bench, including meeting agendas.

She does not perform mystery-genius theatrics. Her skill is disciplined comparison: what would be different if each candidate were true, and which safe action can expose that difference? She can be difficult to read because she expresses care by making another person's explanation stronger.

Her contradiction is a high standard for Isolation that can become operationally expensive. She must learn that uncertainty can be documented and bounded without being eliminated completely.

**Relationship to the loop:** Hypothesize, Test interpretation, Isolate, causal chains, and the difference between actionable and deepest root cause.

**Campaign pressure:** a formal analysis that implicates a test profile, client condition, or reclaimed-parts lot carries consequences beyond one unit.

**Portrait hook:** forties; close-cropped hair; magnifying visor pushed up; slim notebook crowded with diagrams; violet antistatic mat and trace-like background lines; plum accent.

**Voice sample:** “Possible is cheap. Show me the observation that makes this possibility different from the others.”

### Luis Ortega — senior board-repair technician

**Home function:** Bench<br>
**Story role:** connects causal claims to physical work and refuses the false choice between craft and analysis

Luis has repaired boards long enough to distrust both swagger and despair. He can replace a fine-pitch component, but he is equally willing to stop before touching a board when the evidence does not support the work. He teaches with his hands visible and names every point where a repair can introduce a new fault.

He is warm, patient, and protective of junior technicians. His blind spot is a tendency to repair around bad processes because he knows he can. That competence sometimes hides systemic problems from the metrics.

**Relationship to the loop:** Repair prerequisites, workmanship, substitution as both intervention and Evidence, and the warning that a successful repair does not retroactively prove a clean Isolation.

**Campaign pressure:** parts scarcity and contract deadlines reward bundled interventions that make attribution harder.

**Portrait hook:** late forties; repair apron with neatly arranged hand tools; magnification lenses; one hand holding a board by safe edges; warm bench light; amber accent.

**Voice sample:** “I can make the symptom leave. You still have to tell Gate why it will stay gone.”

### Hana Park — Total Quality auditor

**Home function:** Validation Gate<br>
**Story role:** apparent obstacle who becomes the clearest advocate for the end user and the integrity of the record

Hana independently checks function, workmanship, identity, accessories, and documentation. She rejects units people have emotionally finished. On a hurried floor, that can make her look antagonistic even when the rejection prevents a repeat return.

She is exact without being humorless. She collects absurd euphemisms for missing evidence—“administratively healthy” is a favorite—and writes rejection notes no one can plausibly misunderstand. Her contradiction is that audit sampling and quotas force her to accept that quality cannot inspect every truth back into a process.

**Relationship to the loop:** Verify, Documentation completeness, release criteria, immutable history, and reopening Diagnosis when required state is not proven.

**Campaign pressure:** a public contract milestone creates pressure to reduce audit depth just as Civic Atlas repeaters increase.

**Portrait hook:** late thirties; clean light-gray quality coat; tablet displaying a traveler checklist; small green release tag clipped beside a red hold tag; upright composition; green and white accent.

**Voice sample:** “I believe you fixed something. The record has not told me what, and the unit has not proved enough.”

### Beatrice “Bea” Morgan — Outflow coordinator

**Home function:** Packing, fulfillment, and dispatch<br>
**Story role:** closes the physical loop and notices when a machine the dashboard calls complete comes back through another door

Bea thinks spatially. She knows which carrier mishandles a particular chassis size, which client rejects mixed accessory lots, and which destination will return a unit over one absent rail kit. She keeps a private map of where machines go because the official status “shipped” tells her almost nothing.

She is sociable and seems less technical than the Core crew until a destination pattern breaks a case open. Her blind spot is informal knowledge: what she remembers should have entered the Worklog or logistics system earlier.

**Relationship to the loop:** final identity reconciliation, packaging, Document consequences, repeat-return detection, and evidence that closure is not the end of a machine's story.

**Campaign pressure:** Outflow is blamed for delays created upstream and praised for shipping units whose quality it cannot independently establish.

**Portrait hook:** early thirties; dispatch vest with a marker tucked behind one ear; label roll and destination map; freight lights in the background; magenta and indigo accent.

**Voice sample:** “Closed is a status. Delivered is a location. Working is something the next person gets to decide.”

### Priya Nayar — Civic Atlas program manager

**Home function:** Client Programs<br>
**Story role:** carries the client's stakes into the building and the building's findings back out

Priya negotiates scope, cost, confidentiality, release criteria, and what counts as a defensible answer. She is not a corporate villain. Civic Atlas serves organizations that cannot simply wait for perfect information, and every added test competes with a system someone needs back.

She is persuasive because she can translate between technical, contractual, and public language. Her contradiction is that translation can become smoothing: an uncomfortable uncertainty may emerge from a meeting sounding more settled than it was.

**Relationship to the loop:** reported symptoms, documentation audience, verification requirements, client-provided context, and the consequences of a root-cause statement.

**Campaign pressure:** the contract can establish Second Current as a trusted public-infrastructure partner or expose a pattern neither organization wants attached to its name.

**Portrait hook:** forties; client-facing jacket over practical floor shoes; transparent display with a lot summary; one damaged shipping label retained in a document sleeve; burgundy accent.

**Voice sample:** “Give me the uncertainty in words I can carry into a room that has already promised a date.”

### Jonah Reed — Knowledge Systems analyst

**Home function:** Worklog integrations and SIFT stewardship<br>
**Story role:** personifies responsibility for an automated system without turning that system into a person or an all-purpose antagonist

Jonah began as a test technician and moved into data systems after discovering how much result context disappeared between a rig and a report. He maintains SIFT's integrations, evaluation sets, and recommendation display. He knows the tool's limitations, but repeated exposure to bad criticism has made him reflexively defend it.

His contradiction is central to the campaign: SIFT can reveal patterns no one technician can hold, yet its confidence is shaped by selective records produced under operational pressure. Jonah must distinguish defending the value of the system from defending every answer it produces.

**Relationship to the loop:** Evidence provenance, Worklog chronology, recommendation visibility, player-safe information, and the institutional effect of Documentation.

**Campaign pressure:** leadership wants SIFT featured at the sustainability showcase, while the rotation uncovers gaps in its training history and evaluation assumptions.

**Portrait hook:** mid-thirties; split setting between an old Rigline badge and a current systems console; repair-history timelines rather than glowing AI imagery; cyan accent restrained by neutral grays.

**Voice sample:** “It did not invent the confidence. We taught it what our records make confidence look like.”

## Relationship lines

- Ev recruited Sora into the rotation design, but they disagree about how much uncertainty operations can afford.
- Inez and Bea exchange repeat-return information informally; the campaign reveals both the value and fragility of that habit.
- Malik and Hana have a productive rivalry over the difference between a test-profile pass and a release decision.
- Luis has quietly fixed fixture damage for Rigline more than once, which protects the shift and obscures the maintenance trend.
- Priya and Ev respect one another, but each is responsible to a different definition of “complete.”
- Sora distrusts SIFT's displayed certainty; Jonah distrusts critiques that ignore its calibrated usefulness. Neither is wholly right.
- The player becomes the only person whose assigned role is to keep these relationships in the same causal picture.

## Secondary-character spaces

Later episodes can add recurring people without crowding the initial ensemble:

- a Materials analyst who owns quarantined and reclaimed part lots;
- a Reclamation technician who sees value and risk in every irreparable unit;
- a night-shift lead whose Worklogs are the day shift's only contact with them;
- a remote Global Desk specialist with different tools and client norms;
- a Civic Atlas field technician who knows conditions the return form did not capture;
- trainees and computer-controlled technicians used in simulations and cooperative play;
- friends, guild contacts, and room participants outside campaign canon.

Every addition should connect two or more parts of the world rather than exist only to dispense a card.

## Portrait-production boundary

When portraits are commissioned:

- define one shared framing, light logic, uniform system, and background-language guide first;
- preserve varied ages, skin tones, body types, hair, gender expression, and assistive devices;
- distinguish teams through tools, safety wear, environment, and accent color rather than stereotypes;
- create neutral, focused, amused, concerned, and relieved expressions before elaborate poses;
- keep player customization cosmetically expressive and mechanically separate unless a later rule explicitly says otherwise.
