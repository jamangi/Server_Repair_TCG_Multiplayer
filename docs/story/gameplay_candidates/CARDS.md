# Story-derived card candidates

Status: **narrative and design prompts only; no approved cards, IDs, costs, rarities, or effects**

These concepts connect the Second Current workplace to the existing card families. They should reuse current domain objects where coverage exists and enter domain or decision review where it does not. A story name is not permission to create a duplicate entity.

## Card-fiction principle

The player deck represents a prepared **shift repertoire**:

- procedures and Commands represented by owned cards;
- tools, fixtures, and known-good resources the technician can access;
- compatible parts or component options;
- trained workflows and reference practices;
- limited help from colleagues or organizational systems.

Cards need not be literal objects in the fiction. Drawing one means an option has become usable under the match's limits of time, attention, access, and inventory. The first-version draw, discard, Document Live recovery, Search, and Refresh meanings are frozen; card-specific effects and campaign reward balance remain candidates.

Named cast members should not automatically become collectible power objects. A relationship can unlock a procedure or scene without turning the person into an inventory item. If Technician cards are adopted later, separate cosmetic identity, campaign relationship, deck-building permission, and mechanical effect.

## Campaign unlock candidates

### Rotation completion

Finishing a team chapter may award new cards through an explicit campaign reward and may separately display an honor-only Qualification badge. The reward source and badge are distinct: the badge never grants the cards or changes their legality.

### Demonstrated practice

A mentor unlocks a method after the player encounters the underlying reasoning in play. This should not require one exact perfect line if several technically valid solutions exist.

### Worklog discovery

A well-preserved prior case introduces a workflow, Test, or repair concept. The reward makes Documentation feel institutionally useful without deciding its in-match economy.

### Client authorization

A campaign contract temporarily permits access to a vendor tool, history set, fixture, or procedure. Client-specific access should not make a universally legal deck unusable outside the campaign.

### Relationship trust

A character offers consultation, a loaned tool, or advanced training after story choices. Avoid equating friendship points directly with technical truth.

## Existing-content discipline

Before creating any card from this list:

1. search the current Components, Tools, Tests, Commands, Repair Procedures, Validation Procedures, Protocols, and Workflow concepts;
2. reference an existing entity when the game function is equivalent;
3. treat vendor or fictional names as presentation aliases unless they add real mechanics;
4. send genuine domain gaps through schema and content review;
5. resolve event, visibility, Isolation, Documentation, and economy questions before writing final effects;
6. preserve stable entity IDs and do not derive a new ID from the working story name.

## Cards this story does not need

- generic “Observe,” “Think,” or “Make a Hypothesis” cards required to use ordinary reasoning;
- attack cards that sabotage another technician or damage a client unit;
- an omniscient SIFT card that reads hidden faults;
- a “Repair Complete” card that bypasses Verify;
- a “Paperwork” card whose joke is that Documentation has no technical value;
- character cards that turn ethnicity, gender expression, age, or disability into power ratings;
- future-tech cards that replace rather than teach the real troubleshooting concepts in the domain layer.
