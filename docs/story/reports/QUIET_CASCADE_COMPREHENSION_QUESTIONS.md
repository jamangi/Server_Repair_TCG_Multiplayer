# Quiet Cascade cold-reader comprehension questions

These questions test only public workplace and process context. Expected answers deliberately omit hidden Faults, required diagnostics, correct Repairs, and unchosen Match outcomes. TASK-034’s candidate transcripts must answer them without consulting character sheets, the campaign blueprint, or implementation code.

## Chapter 1 — Learn the Line

1. **What does Second Current do at Trinity Hub, and what is your role?**
   Expected public-context answer: Second Current returns equipment to service at Trinity Hub. You are a capable new First Look technician in a rotation that follows information across team handoffs.

2. **What does Inflow record before First Look triage?**
   Expected public-context answer: Identity, custody route, packaging condition, accessories, and the client complaint—not a technical diagnosis.

3. **How do the traveler and Worklog differ?**
   Expected public-context answer: The traveler follows identity, routing, accessories, and release checks; the Worklog preserves the official chronology of accepted service work and results.

4. **What must happen between a Hypothesis and release?**
   Expected public-context answer: Tests must create Ticket-specific Evidence, accepted Isolation must support Repair, Verify must independently prove the named required state, and Documentation must preserve the path for Gate.

5. **What remains unknown before each Match?**
   Expected public-context answer: The underlying fault, decisive diagnostic route, correct Repair, and outcome remain unknown until authorized gameplay establishes or reveals them.

## Chapter 2 — Follow the Repeaters

1. **What do Rigline, Trace, and Bench each contribute?**
   Expected public-context answer: Rigline reproduces under named conditions, Trace designs discriminating comparisons and supports Isolation, and Bench performs supported state changes and reports what changed.

2. **Why are fixture, target, and condition attached to a result?**
   Expected public-context answer: They show which setup produced the result and which question it actually answered, so a later reader does not overgeneralize a pass or failure.

3. **What does “repeater” mean at Trinity Hub?**
   Expected public-context answer: A unit that returns after release. The campaign has one confirmed repeat serial and a broader repeated handoff habit, not one proven repeating technical fault.

4. **Why can similar memory or thermal complaints need separate explanations?**
   Expected public-context answer: A shared subsystem or symptom does not transfer Evidence, Isolation, Repair, or Verify between Tickets.

5. **What can the Player do next with this context?**
   Expected public-context answer: Choose whether reproduction or discrimination leads, then preserve the chosen purpose and conditions in the Worklog.

## Chapter 3 — Read Between Worklogs

1. **What does Knowledge Systems maintain, and what is SIFT?**
   Expected public-context answer: Knowledge Systems maintains Worklogs, integrations, and search. SIFT is a service-record search and recommendation tool limited to authorized sources; it cannot recover omissions or decide what an old result proves now.

2. **Which fields must survive with a test result?**
   Expected public-context answer: Its source, target, operating condition, output, and time, plus the later interpretation as a distinct step.

3. **What can a negative result establish?**
   Expected public-context answer: Only the branch it actually tested; it can narrow a Candidate without proving every alternative or turning missing data into Evidence.

4. **Why separate link-state and address-configuration Evidence?**
   Expected public-context answer: They answer different network questions, and each Ticket needs its own current Evidence before Isolation.

5. **What is the quiet cascade?**
   Expected public-context answer: An organizational pattern in which independent faults become harder to explain when source context is lost across handoffs—not a shared hidden hardware fault.

## Chapter 4 — Put the Truth in Service

1. **What does Client Programs do with the final account?**
   Expected public-context answer: It translates supported outcomes, scope, timing, release bounds, and remaining risk into decisions Civic Atlas can act on.

2. **What changes when the Player chooses outcomes-first or uncertainty-first?**
   Expected public-context answer: Only rhetorical order and acknowledgment change; authorized facts, bounds, Match configuration, and outcome band do not.

3. **How should the final three-Ticket queue be handled?**
   Expected public-context answer: Coordinate resources across the queue while keeping each Ticket’s Evidence, Isolation, Repair, Verify, and Worklog independent.

4. **What do the three ending bands authorize?**
   Expected public-context answer: A client-facing reviewed release, a bounded account of closed work and open risk, or a Gate hold for continued review; none rewrites Match truth.

5. **What still remains unknown after the campaign’s organizational conclusion?**
   Expected public-context answer: No common supplier defect, field condition, component failure, or shared technical root has been established; unresolved Tickets remain bounded to their own records.
