# Character voice-translation cards

Status: **TASK-035 production guidance; examples derive from TASK-034's context-complete draft**

Each exercise begins from the cited candidate's complete semantic payload. The translated example may be used or revised by TASK-036, but the payload floor and drift warning remain authoritative. Examples do not add game state.

## Inez Calder

**Fingerprint:** Fast physical inventory plus a clean inference boundary. Inez pairs concrete contrasts, invites another look, and makes humor from objects or routes—not from a client's loss.

**Preferred:** paired observations; active verbs; quick questions; “this tells us / this does not tell us”; object-first sequence. **Avoided:** abstract process slogans, unearned certainty, long nominal phrases, and jokes at a learner's expense.

- **Ordinary floor:** brisk, visual, companionable; a fact often arrives before its label.
- **Pressure/conflict:** enumerates custody facts, reduces teasing, and marks observation versus inference explicitly.
- **Reflective/debrief:** admits when pattern recognition outran explanation and gives the next handoff a reconstruction.

**Translation exercise 1 — `candidate.qc01.v2.context.inflow.01`:** Payload floor: Inflow matches serial, custody route, packaging condition, accessories, and client complaint to the traveler before First Look triage. Translation: “Before First Look gets the machine, I marry five things to the traveler: serial, route, box condition, accessories, and the complaint. Miss one and the next team inherits a mystery we made.”

**Translation exercise 2 — `candidate.qc01.v2.context.records.01`:** Payload floor: traveler covers identity/routing/accessories/release checks; Worklog is official chronology of accepted actions/results/conclusions trusted by later teams. Translation: “Traveler for where it went and what went with it. Worklog for who did what, what came back, and what we accepted. Different histories; later teams need both to hold.”

**Bad generic version:** “Inflow processes logistics records before triage and all documentation must be accurate.” It fails because it erases the five required fields, collapses traveler and Worklog, hides Inez's physical attention, and sounds interchangeable with policy copy.

**Semantic-drift warning:** Do not let packaging condition prove a fault, make an informal memory official Evidence, or imply First Look receives less than all five named fields.

**Relational exchange:** Inez: “New seal, old dent, two route labels.” Ev: “What does that establish?” Inez: “A custody question. Not a fault. I know the difference; I just get there faster than my explanation.”

## Ev Shaw

**Fingerprint:** Consequence-oriented questions, compressed distinctions, and silence that asks another person to own the reasoning. Ev names the authority boundary before the inspirational promise.

**Preferred:** short questions; balanced contrasts; verbs with owners; decisions framed by what survives a handoff. **Avoided:** motivational speeches, ornamental metaphor, false consensus, and withholding context needed for safe action.

- **Ordinary floor:** brief, plain, curious; asks for changed state and evidence.
- **Pressure/conflict:** shorter clauses, explicit tradeoffs, sometimes too little social cushioning.
- **Reflective/debrief:** reveals institutional consequence, admits protected context, and connects local work to durable authority.

**Translation exercise 1 — `candidate.qc01.v2.context.lifecycle.01`:** Payload floor: a Ticket is one unit's service case; mark a plausible Candidate as Hypothesis; Tests gather Evidence that separates alternatives. Translation: “One unit, one Ticket. Which plausible Candidate are you willing to call the Hypothesis? Good. Now choose a Test that can separate it from the rest; activity is not Evidence by itself.”

**Translation exercise 2 — `candidate.qc01.v2.context.repeaters.01`:** Payload floor: repeater means a unit returned after release, not repeated symptom/device; one confirmed serial; broader repetition is a handoff habit, not proven common technical cause. Translation: “Define repeater before the word starts doing our thinking. Here it means a unit returned after release. We have one confirmed serial and a repeated handoff habit—not a repeated symptom, a radio device, or one proven cause.”

**Bad generic version:** “Follow the troubleshooting process carefully and avoid assumptions.” It fails because it names no lifecycle step, decision, alternative, or consequence and removes Ev's demand that reasoning survive transfer.

**Semantic-drift warning:** Do not make a Candidate a fact, a Test automatically Evidence, a repeater a symptom, or the procedural pattern a common Fault.

**Relational exchange:** Ev: “Can Gate defend it?” Priya: “Not in that wording.” Ev: “Then the wording is not our last problem. Show me the unsupported promise underneath it.”

## Malik Okoye

**Fingerprint:** Demonstration-minded precision. Malik likes apparatus, defines the question a rig actually asked, and uses orderly examples or label humor to separate reproducible from relevant.

**Preferred:** condition-first explanations; parallel examples; named fixtures/targets; invitations to challenge a profile. **Avoided:** mystical machines, green-badge certainty, contempt for manual work, and technobabble without an observable consequence.

- **Ordinary floor:** enthusiastic, explanatory, lightly playful about cables and labels.
- **Pressure/conflict:** literal, control-oriented, and defensive about reproducibility before he catches himself.
- **Reflective/debrief:** names the assumption he protected and publishes a limit beside the result.

**Translation exercise 1 — `text.qc01.ch02.rigline.01`:** Payload floor: a fixture is rig/setup; a result without target and operating condition has little use. Translation: “A fixture is the rig plus the setup that made the result. Lose the target and operating condition and you have a very well-labeled souvenir. Yes, I own a label maker; no, it cannot restore missing context.”

**Translation exercise 2 — `text.qc01.ch02.shift04.01`:** Payload floor: both thermal units pass a short idle profile and exceed expected range under sustained processor work. Translation: “Same shape on both records: short idle stays inside range; sustained processor load climbs past it. The rig reproduced that change. It has not named what caused it.”

**Bad generic version:** “Testing shows a thermal issue under load.” It fails because it drops both units, idle control, expected range, sustained processor condition, and the limit between reproduction and cause.

**Semantic-drift warning:** Reproducibility does not establish relevance or cause; a fixture/result must retain target and condition, and the two Tickets remain independent.

**Relational exchange:** Hana: “Your green badge has no duration.” Malik: “It has a duration in the profile.” Hana: “Not in the record Gate received.” Malik: “Then the record is wrong—and I should have noticed before defending the badge.”

## Sora Chen

**Fingerprint:** Conditional comparison. Sora asks what would differ if each Candidate were true, treats a useful negative as information, and expresses care by strengthening someone else's claim.

**Preferred:** counterfactual questions; exact qualifiers; asymmetry; bounded unknowns; compact spatial metaphors. **Avoided:** genius reveals, theatrical certainty, endless analysis without a safe next action, and using coldness as proof of intelligence.

- **Ordinary floor:** quiet comparisons and practical next questions.
- **Pressure/conflict:** colder, narrower, more explicit about what the record cannot carry.
- **Reflective/debrief:** acknowledges operational cost and explains how an incomplete model can still support bounded action.

**Translation exercise 1 — `text.qc01.ch02.trace.02`:** Payload floor: a discriminating Test may be negative and still narrow Candidates if target and condition stay attached. Translation: “Negative is not empty. If the Test reached the named target under the named condition, it can close one branch of the tree. Detach either, and we no longer know which branch moved.”

**Translation exercise 2 — `candidate.qc01.v2.context.specialists.01`:** Payload floor: Bench performs supported physical/configuration change; handoff justifies work; Bench tells Gate what changed and may have been disturbed. Translation: “Bench changes physical or configured state. We owe Luis the Evidence that makes the change supportable; Bench owes Gate the exact change and what it could have disturbed. Skip either half and a successful symptom becomes an incomplete explanation.”

**Bad generic version:** “Run more tests before repair and document everything.” It fails because more data is not necessarily discriminating, the handoff has named responsibilities, and “everything” does not identify disturbance or source conditions.

**Semantic-drift warning:** A negative result excludes only what it actually tested; Bench does not create Isolation, and successful Repair does not retroactively prove the causal claim.

**Relational exchange:** Jonah: “SIFT found the pattern.” Sora: “It displayed a pattern.” Jonah: “That distinction is doing a lot of work.” Sora: “So is your system. Let both claims keep their proper size.”

## Hana Park

**Fingerprint:** Auditable distinctions with exact ownership. Hana turns a rejection into a remediable next state and lets dry humor puncture euphemisms, not people.

**Preferred:** parallel clauses; explicit actors; release criteria; “current” and “independent” when required; bounded remediation. **Avoided:** “basically fixed,” passive missing evidence, moral judgment of a learner, and humor that obscures a hold.

- **Ordinary floor:** direct, checkable, occasionally wry.
- **Pressure/conflict:** formal, actor-specific, with little tolerance for verbs that hide ownership.
- **Reflective/debrief:** acknowledges effort, explains why independence matters, and names the process gap rather than a villain.

**Translation exercise 1 — `candidate.qc01.v2.context.points.success.01`:** Payload floor: Story Service Points summarize closure-settled Isolation and necessary Repair contributions; Tests/Verify/Documentation/closure essential but add no separate points. Translation: “Story Service Points count only closure-settled Isolation and necessary Repair contributions. Tests, Verify, Documentation, and closure are still required. Essential is not the same as separately scored; please spare me ‘administratively optional.’”

**Translation exercise 2 — `text.qc01.ch01.shift01.success.01`:** Payload floor: Gate has current Verify of required state plus Worklog naming supported change; unit may leave review. Translation: “Gate has two things it can audit: a current, independent Verify of the required state and a Worklog that names the supported change. This unit may leave review.”

**Bad generic version:** “Quality has approved the repair and awarded points.” It fails because Gate verifies a required state rather than blessing a repair, drops Documentation and independence, and invents point authority.

**Semantic-drift warning:** Never make a Gate pass prove root cause, award points outside closure settlement, or imply unscored Tests/Verify/Documentation are optional.

**Relational exchange:** Malik: “The profile passed.” Hana: “Which required state did it verify?” Malik: “You collect that question.” Hana: “Only because ‘administratively healthy’ keeps trying to escape into public records.”

## Jonah Reed

**Fingerprint:** Provenance chain in source-to-transformation-to-display order. Jonah qualifies system verbs, opens the record beneath a summary, and becomes most distinctive when he corrects his own defensiveness.

**Preferred:** attributable verbs; field sequences; index/map analogies; permission bounds; explicit system limitations. **Avoided:** SIFT as a person, “the data says,” recommendation as diagnosis, magical recovery of omitted context, and privacy jokes.

- **Ordinary floor:** layered explanation with a source chain and mild interface self-deprecation.
- **Pressure/conflict:** faster, field-heavy, reflexively defensive until he separates stewardship from output.
- **Reflective/debrief:** distinguishes what SIFT did, what the record allowed, and what he wanted the result to mean.

**Translation exercise 1 — `text.qc01.ch03.entry.02`:** Payload floor: SIFT searches/recommends from authorized words, serials, outcomes, dates; cannot recover omitted provenance or decide what an old result proves now. Translation: “SIFT indexes authorized words, serials, outcomes, and dates, then recommends related records. It cannot reconstruct a field nobody kept, and it cannot decide what an old result proves about this unit now. I maintain a map, not an oracle.”

**Translation exercise 2 — `candidate.qc01.v2.context.sift.01`:** Payload floor: rig normally publishes source/target/condition/output/time; missing fields let SIFT repeat conclusion but prevent next technician knowing question answered. Translation: “Normally the rig publishes source, target, condition, output, and time. Strip those fields and SIFT can still echo the conclusion—cleanly, unfortunately—but the next technician cannot reconstruct the question.”

**Bad generic version:** “AI found a likely issue in the historical data.” It fails because SIFT is not AI authority here, no issue was diagnosed, the authorized inputs vanish, and missing provenance becomes invented knowledge.

**Semantic-drift warning:** SIFT may surface or recommend authorized records; it cannot reveal hidden Ticket truth, manufacture omitted Evidence, diagnose a machine, or determine current causality.

**Relational exchange:** Sora: “The display makes the bound easy to miss.” Jonah: “The bound is in the source.” Sora: “The next technician sees the display first.” Jonah: “Then that is my design problem, not their reading failure.”

## Priya Nayar

**Fingerprint:** Audience-first translation with accountable modal verbs. Priya leads with the decision a record enables, then protects—or under pressure risks smoothing—the exact bound it must carry.

**Preferred:** scope/risk/date/owner; useful outcomes followed by explicit exclusions; “can,” “cannot,” and “remains”; language that can travel unchanged. **Avoided:** villainous corporate shorthand, false certainty for elegance, unowned promises, and technical metaphor in liability-sensitive sentences.

- **Ordinary floor:** polished but practical, asks what the authorized record permits.
- **Pressure/conflict:** formally gracious, verb-focused, tempted to treat resistance as a wording problem.
- **Reflective/debrief:** direct about tradeoffs and proud when a difficult limit survives compression.

**Translation exercise 1 — `candidate.qc01.v2.context.client.01`:** Payload floor: Civic Atlas uses account for return-to-service/review/uncertainty-to-operators decisions; smooth wording cannot exceed Gate/Worklogs. Translation: “Civic Atlas will use this account to decide what returns to service, what stays under review, and which uncertainty follows the unit to its operators. Make it readable, yes. Do not make it smoother than Gate and the Worklogs permit.”

**Translation exercise 2 — `text.qc01.ch04.ending.bounded.01`:** Payload floor: Bounded Account separates closure-valid work, private archived outcomes, and unverified operating conditions. Translation: “Bounded Account. The client gets three clearly separated sets: closure-valid work, private archived outcomes, and operating conditions that remain unverified. Useful does not require pretending those sets are one.”

**Bad generic version:** “We need a clear client summary that balances risk and business needs.” It fails because it names no client decisions, erases Gate and Worklog authority, and can hide the three required outcome categories.

**Semantic-drift warning:** Readability cannot widen authorization; a client account cannot turn private archived answers into public evidence, collapse separate causes, or call an unverified queue released.

**Relational exchange:** Priya: “I can carry that sentence into the review.” Ev: “Can you carry its limit too?” Priya: “If I cannot, the sentence is not ready. Give me the narrower verb.”
